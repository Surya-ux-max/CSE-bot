## REGULATION AND FRAMEWORK — CSE CURRICULUM

Category: Curriculum FAQ
Department: Computer Science and Engineering (CSE)

Summary:
The CSE curriculum follows Regulation 2023, uses the Outcome-Based Education framework, operates under the Choice Based Credit System, spans eight semesters over four years, and requires 163 credits to graduate.

Description:
The Computer Science and Engineering curriculum follows Regulation 2023. It is built on the Outcome-Based Education (OBE) framework, which focuses on the knowledge and skills students acquire by graduation. The credit system used is the Choice Based Credit System (CBCS), which provides flexibility in choosing elective courses. The programme runs for four years divided into eight semesters and requires a total of 163 credits for successful completion.

Every semester includes laboratory components where applicable. The curriculum also includes professional development, mandatory courses, internships, and project phases.

## SEMESTER I SUBJECTS — C PROGRAMMING AND MATHEMATICS

Category: Curriculum FAQ
Department: Computer Science and Engineering (CSE)

Summary:
Semester I covers Calculus and Linear Algebra, Problem Solving using C, Technical English, Physics for Information Science, Application Design and Development, with labs in C Programming and Engineering Practices.

Description:
The first semester of the CSE programme (Semester I) introduces students to the fundamentals of engineering. The core subjects are Calculus and Linear Algebra, Problem Solving using C, Technical English, Physics for Information Science, and Application Design and Development. The laboratories include Problem Solving using C Laboratory and Engineering Practices Laboratory. The professional development activity is Soft Skills and the mandatory course is Heritage of Tamils.

C Programming is introduced in Semester I through the course Problem Solving using C.

## INTERNSHIP AND PROJECT PHASES

Category: Curriculum FAQ
Department: Computer Science and Engineering (CSE)

Summary:
The CSE programme includes a Summer Internship in Semester V, Project Phase I in Semester VII, and Project Phase II (the final year capstone project) in Semester VIII.

Description:
The curriculum includes three major experiential learning components.

The Summer Internship is completed during Semester V or during the break following Semester V. It provides real-world industry exposure to students.

Project Phase I begins in Semester VII. Students identify a research or industry problem, form teams, review literature, and begin development.

Project Phase II is completed in Semester VIII. Students finalize, develop, test, and present their complete project as the capstone of their four-year programme.
